Stitched metal

Naomi Wanjiru

Sheet metal collage

Naomi cuts flattened tins and roofing offcuts into portrait panels, then joins them with a zigzag stitch. She has led the metal bench since the studio opened.

Her story

She started by repairing water tanks with her uncle and kept the offcuts because the printed panels were too good to melt down. The stitching came later, borrowed from her mother's tailoring, and it is now the signature of the metal bench.

Naomi mentors two members each season and runs the cutting safety briefing every Saturday morning.

Painted packaging

Brian Otieno

Acrylic on salvaged sacks

Brian paints street scenes directly onto flattened maize meal sacks, letting the printed branding sit behind the figure. Riders, hawkers and matatus are his usual subjects.

His story

He ran out of canvas during his first year of study and painted on a sack instead. The grain and the folds held the paint better than he expected, and the packaging gave the picture a place and a date without any extra explaining.

Brian handles the studio's commission work and teaches the surface preparation session.

Paper mosaic

Faith Mueni

Seed packets and labels

Faith tiles seed packets, receipts and product labels into dense mosaic fields. Read closely, each piece is a record of what the town bought that month.

Her story

She collects from agrovet shops on the Garissa Road, sorting by colour before anything is cut. Her rule is that every packet keeps at least one readable word, so the source is never hidden by the composition.

Faith keeps the studio material library and knows exactly what is in stock.

Reclaimed timber

Kevin Kimani

Furniture and carved panels

Kevin works pallet wood and demolition timber into stools, frames and carved wall panels. He finishes everything with oil rather than paint so the old marks stay visible.

His story

Trained as a carpenter on building sites, he grew tired of watching good timber burned at the end of a job. Now the same sites call him first, and the nail holes and paint lines become part of the design.

Kevin builds the studio's own benches, plinths and exhibition crates.

Textile offcuts

Aisha Hassan

Assemblage and soft sculpture

Aisha gathers fabric ends from tailoring shops and builds them into layered wall hangings and soft sculpture. Colour is sorted by hand before a single stitch.

Her story

She learned to sew before she learned to draw, and still thinks in seams. The offcut bags she collects each week would otherwise go to landfill within a day of the garments being finished.

Aisha runs the studio's Saturday session for younger participants.

Bottle tops

Samuel Njoroge

Plastic and metal caps

Samuel builds pattern fields and standing figures from bottle tops, washers and crushed plastic. Bars and kiosks across Thika save their caps for him.

His story

He started counting caps as a way to teach his younger cousins numbers, then noticed the colours already formed a palette. A single large panel can hold more than four thousand of them.

Samuel coordinates the collection routes and the weekly material weigh in.
