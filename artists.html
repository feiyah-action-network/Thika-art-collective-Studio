Dennis Bull Ndegwa

Acrylic on repurposed unga paper and canvas

Profile

Born in Nairobi in 1998, with more than five years in Kenya's art scene. In his words, his practice is rooted in capturing the rhythms, resilience and resourcefulness of everyday Kenyan life, and his art echoes both the vibrancy and the grit of his surroundings.

His recent work combines acrylics with recycled unga paper, drawing on bodabodas as symbolic vessels for Kenya's economic shifts, urban motion and the grit of people hustling for their daily bread, or kutafuta unga as he puts it. Through those layered surfaces he explores survival, innovation and the hustle that defines Kenya's social and cultural fabric. He signs his work Bull.

George Kamiti

Stitched metal, salvaged packaging and leather

Profile

He cuts sheet metal, flattened tin labels, packaging and leather to shape, scorches them, and joins them on a sewing machine. The stitch line is left visible, so every piece is drawn twice: once by the cut edge and once by the thread that holds it down. Faces come out of scorched panels, and hair out of whatever grid the packaging offers, seed packets, tomato paste labels, netting, dotted paper.

Twenty eight of his pieces are in the gallery. Most are wall work, but the G-avants series is not: those are wearable, leather bodices built up with brass rosettes, shell, beadwork and salvaged fittings. Titles are his own, from the folder he supplied. No medium, size or year came with them.

John Ruitha Maina

Acrylic on salvaged cardboard and packaging

Profile

He is 27 and works from his home studio in Thika Town. He began with oil paints and moved to acrylics, which he says he finds more enjoyable to work with, and describes his style as a modern one built on urban narratives, human figures, emotions and contemporary fashion. He is self taught, and says he keeps learning from artists at home and abroad.

Since early 2023 he has been painting on cardboard. In his words the approach seeks to raise awareness about recycling, since a great deal of cardboard is thrown away and only a small fraction of it is recycled or upcycled. He collects it from bins, from shops and from friends' homes, so the surface of a painting is made of the things people around him have already bought and finished with, which is the point he is making about consumerism. He signs his work Ruitha.

Daniel Kabiaru

Acrylic on cardboard and canvas, and cardboard sculpture

Profile

He works in two directions at once. One is paint on torn corrugated cardboard, a single figure from the street set against poured black and white paint and a hard band of colour. The other is canvas, where the same figures dissolve into curling ribbons of colour. He signs his work Dan Kabiaru.

Thirty three of his pieces are in the gallery, dated 2024 to 2026, with the titles, mediums and sizes he records for each one. They include one sculpture, a seated figure built entirely from rolled cardboard.

Peter Ndirangu

Acrylic in a woven like technique, portraits and murals

Profile

A Nairobi based visual artist, eleven years in. He describes his work as imaginative composition drawn from personal experience and from the environment around him, and his subjects as domestic and wild animals, the big five in particular.

The style he has been building is a woven like painting technique: the image assembled from thousands of small separate strokes, so a face reads as something threaded together rather than brushed. He works it into portraits as well, and takes murals and commissions.

Record

  • Eight solo exhibitions
  • More than sixteen joint exhibitions, in and around East Africa

Behind the camera

The work has to be seen to be sold.

Paintings made from salvaged cardboard and stitched metal are difficult things to photograph. The surfaces are uneven, half of them catch the light, and a bad picture makes careful work look like a snapshot on a wall. Star takes the pictures.

Stacey Waithera Kamiti

Photography, photo editing and video. Known as Star.

She is a Journalism and Applied Communication student at Multimedia University of Kenya, with two years of practical experience in creative media and communication. She holds a Diploma in Journalism and is working towards a Bachelor's degree in Applied Communication.

In her own words the thread running through all of it is visual storytelling: using pictures to communicate an idea, document an experience and make something people actually engage with. Creative, curious and adaptable is how she puts it, and the part she keeps coming back to is capturing a moment that means something and telling it so it reaches people.
